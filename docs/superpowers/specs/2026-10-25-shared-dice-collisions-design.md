# Collisions between all visible dice

The user requested physical collisions between rolling dice. The existing Cannon simulation already collides dice inside one roll, but the renderer plays separate physics worlds for separate rolls. Dice from different clicks can therefore pass through each other.

Use one shared recorded simulation for every visible die. When dice arrive, continue existing dice from their current position, orientation, velocity, angular velocity and sleeping state, then simulate them together with the new dice. Reuse existing meshes/textures and keep their face labels fixed. A new impact can wake or turn over an older die, as on a physical table; its already-recorded gameplay result remains unchanged in history. Assign new dice’s labels before their first frame so the new roll’s settled faces match its recorded results. The renderer’s top-face metadata must reflect actual fixed labels after older dice are bumped.

Approaches: a fully live engine would require a new result/face-settlement mechanism; a continuation of the existing precomputed physics gives real rigid-body impulses without changing gameplay randomness or replacing the rendering engine. Choose shared continuation as the smallest change consistent with the existing design. Combine queued lazy-load rolls into one simulation, retaining per-die styles. At most 18 visible/physical dice; remove older bodies with their meshes. Retain one canvas/context, idle reuse, correct group settling/fade timing, hidden-tab pausing, resize behavior, reduced motion and no-WebGL fallback. No dependencies or changes to random results, totals or history.

Tests must prove physical trajectory/impulse changes for cross-roll impacts, sleeping dice wake on contact, continuation starts at the sampled pose/velocities, and a burst of separate one-die rolls produces collisions in the actual renderer. Cover mixed shapes/radii, narrow screens, pruning and lazy-load bursts. Inspect the animation visually. Deliver a reviewable PR without merging until requested.
